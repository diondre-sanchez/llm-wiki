# The Giver's Burden

## Definition

The Giver's Burden describes the specific dynamic where individuals who are "always there for others" possess "an ocean for a heart" but risk their own well-being. It characterizes the emotional labor and exhaustion inherent in deep givers who often fall for partners "too afraid to swim," thereby neglecting their own need for rest and being held [[A Gentle Reminder]].

## How it works

This dynamic manifests when a person's capacity for deep care becomes a liability in relationships. The giver offers immense emotional depth and availability, but this often attracts partners who are unable or unwilling to reciprocate that depth. The result is a one-sided flow of energy where the giver's needs for support and validation are consistently overlooked in favor of their role as the primary caregiver [[A Gentle Reminder]].

## Examples

- **The "Ocean" Metaphor:** The text uses the image of having "an ocean for a heart" to illustrate the vastness of the giver's capacity, contrasted with partners who are "too afraid to swim" in those depths [[A Gentle Reminder]].
- **Risk of Neglect:** Givers risk their own need for rest and being held, often prioritizing the protection of others at all costs until they become exhausted [[A Gentle Reminder]].
- **Target Audience:** This concept is identified as a recurring theme and a key characteristic of the target audience for works like *A Gentle Reminder*, which speaks directly to those who "protect others at all costs" [[A Gentle Reminder]].
