# Memex

## Definition

Memex is a concept proposed by Vannevar Bush in 1945 describing a personal, curated knowledge store where documents are linked by associative trails. Unlike a traditional library or the later World Wide Web, the Memex is private, actively maintained by its owner, and treats the connections between documents as valuable as the documents themselves [[LLM Wiki Pattern]].

## How it works

In Bush's original vision, the Memex would be a desk-sized device capable of storing a person's books, records, and communications, with a mechanism for creating and following "trails" of associations between items. The core innovation was the associative link: a user could record a path of thought connecting disparate documents, making the trail itself a first-class artifact of knowledge [[LLM Wiki Pattern]].

The LLM Wiki pattern positions itself as a modern realization of this idea. Where Bush's Memex was a theoretical device, the LLM Wiki is a practical system of interlinked markdown files maintained by a large language model. The LLM handles the bookkeeping—updating cross-references, flagging contradictions, and integrating new sources—that Bush identified as the critical maintenance problem [[LLM Wiki Pattern]].

## Examples

- **Original Memex**: A hypothetical 1945 device for storing and associating personal documents, described in Bush's essay "As We May Think" [[LLM Wiki Pattern]].
- **LLM Wiki**: A contemporary implementation where an LLM agent builds and maintains a persistent, interlinked wiki of markdown files, effectively automating the associative trails and maintenance that defined the Memex concept [[LLM Wiki Pattern]].
