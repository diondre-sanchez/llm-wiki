# Self-Closure

## Definition
Self-Closure is the practice of granting oneself permission to let a chapter end unmended, rather than waiting for external validation, apology, or resolution from another person. It is a distinct philosophical stance that positions healing and ending relationships as acts of internal agency rather than outcomes dependent on the other party's cooperation [[A Gentle Reminder]].

## How it works
The concept operates on the premise that "No closure is closure." Traditional closure is often a mechanism for holding on rather than letting go; it does not dissolve pain or patch the soul, but merely adds reasoning to the healing process. By waiting for closure, an individual places the power of their healing in another person’s hands. Self-Closure rejects this dependency by asserting that a half-ending is still an ending, and that loose ends can remain loose without invalidating the experience [[A Gentle Reminder]].

This stance requires the individual to stop giving away their power. Healing, forgiveness, and moving forward are capacities one holds for themselves, not permissions granted by ex-partners. It involves accepting that others may not have been gentle or capable of loving as deserved, and forgiving them for leaving as they did, not for the other person’s sake, but to release resentment [[A Gentle Reminder]].

## Examples
*   **Internal Validation:** Recognizing that "We have to gift ourselves the closure we so deeply desire" and that "That is where the heart mends... And that is something you give yourself" [[A Gentle Reminder]].
*   **Reframing Endings:** Viewing endings as "beautiful, transformative stepping stones" and "cornerstones" of changed chapters, rather than failures requiring external repair [[A Gentle Reminder]].
*   **Autonomy in Grief:** Healing in the "voids and the space" left by others, requiring one to stand in that space and rebuild there, rather than waiting for external resolution [[A Gentle Reminder]].
